## Program framework

**Goal (Impact)**

Over 60,000 financially disadvantage young women and persons with disabilities (PWD) accessing D&F work across the aquaculture value chain, resulting in economic empowerment, resilience and poverty reduction at individual and community levels

**Objectives**

•	Promote gender equity and social inclusion by breaking barriers, supporting shared roles, and ensuring services, institutions, and practices are inclusive.
•	Boost aquaculture output and competitiveness by improving access to essential inputs, lowering start-up costs, and deploying innovation, technology and training to accelerate growth
•	Drive value addition and expand markets for young women through modern processing technologies, stronger linkages to  digital tools, and private-sector investment.
•	Build resilience across the aquaculture ecosystem by investing in leadership, institutions, and networks whiles driving the adoption of better practices, and empowering young women throughout the value chain.

**Assumptions**

•	Sustained domestic fish consumption (estimated at 25kg per capita annually in 2024) will continue to create demand and market for aquaculture products.
•	Private sector actors and partners will continue to invest in production and processing infrastructure and systems, unlocking work opportunities for young women and PWDs.
•	When equipped with relevant skills and supported with targeted productive resources, young people and PWDs will access work opportunities along aquaculture value chain.

### 2. Outcome / 2.1 Work, Enterprise development and Income

**Key result statement:** Increased access to work and enterprise establishment/operations that provides sustainable incomes for young women and persons with disabilities in aquaculture value chains

**Assumptions:**

• 	Partners and anchor firms successfully create and sustain sufficient D&F work opportunities in the aquaculture value chain for SAWA’s target groups.

• 	Participants can pursue these opportunities and retain them, given their caregiving burdens, mobility, and other constraints.

• 	Young women and PWDs are able and willing to take on enterprise leadership roles given their constraints (care burdens, resources, norms).

•	 Business development services, incubation, access to finance and market linkages are sufficient to support enterprise survival and growth.                                                                                         • 	Referred enterprises meet TechnoServe's criteria and funds are disbursed on schedule.

**Risks:**

• 	Market volatility or shocks that reduce demand for fish or fish-related products, thereby affecting work and income.

• 	Safeguarding or workplace issues that could undermine the “dignified” aspect of work if not properly managed.

• 	Persistent barriers for PWDs (access, stigma, and discrimination) if inclusion standards are not fully enforced.

• 	Weak business or financial management capacity leading to enterprise failure despite technical support.

• 	Market shocks or input cost increases that reduce profitability and discourage continued operation.

• 	Barriers to finance or formalization that limit enterprise growth beyond micro scale.                                                                                       • 	Programme-wide TechnoServe targets are not matched by CEL-referred enterprises (attribution to be agreed).

### 2. Outcome / 2.2 Aquaculture Production and Revenue

**Key result statement:** Improved production and revenue generation among programme-supported actors across aquaculture value chains

**Assumptions:**

• 	Partners and anchor firms implement SAWA’s technical standards (site selection, stocking, feed, water quality, biosecurity) consistently, ensuring that production increases are sustainable. 

• 	Market access and pricing remain sufficiently favorable for increased production to translate into revenue, not into surplus with price crashes.

**Risks:**

• 	Environmental shocks (floods, droughts, disease outbreaks) affecting production and survival rates.

• 	Input price volatility (feed, energy) or supply disruptions that undermine profitability.

• 	Market saturation or competition that depresses prices and margins if production increases faster than demand.

### 3. Output / 3.1 Youth accessing starter-pack and Micro-grant

**Key result statement:** Young women and PWDs supported with starter packs and microgrants to access work and enterprise opportunities in aquaculture value chains

**Assumptions:**

•  Starter packs and microgrants are timely, appropriate, adequate, and matched to participants’ employment or enterprise pathways. 

•  Participants have the necessary skills, motivation, and complementary support to use the resources productively and for their intended purposes. 

•  Viable, safe, inclusive, and accessible employment, enterprise, and market opportunities are available. 

•  Participants receive appropriate mentorship, technical assistance, market linkages, and reasonable accommodation. 

•  Household, community, workplace, and institutional conditions support the economic participation of young women and persons with disabilities. 

•  Economic, regulatory, security, and environmental conditions remain conducive to the establishment and continued operation of supported enterprises and employment activities.

**Risks:**

•  Microgrants or starter-pack items may be sold, shared, diverted, or used for purposes unrelated to the approved work or enterprise activity.

•  Participants may struggle to access customers, buyers, employers, suppliers, financial services, and other value-chain actors needed to sustain their work or enterprises.

•  Persons with disabilities and young women may face inaccessible workplaces and equipment, discrimination, harassment, exploitation, or restrictive social and gender norms that limit their participation and control over productive assets and income.

•  Delays in procuring or distributing starter packs and disbursing microgrants affect participants’ ability to take advantage of work or market opportunities.

### 3. Output / 3.2 Training and Skills

**Key result statement:** Young women and PWDs trained and equipped with technical, entrepreneurial and work-readiness skills for employment and enterprise development in aquaculture value chain

**Assumptions:**

•  Training content remains relevant to employment and enterprise opportunities in aquaculture value chains.

•  Participants complete the training and acquire the required technical, entrepreneurial, and work-readiness competencies.

•  Training facilities, materials, equipment, and delivery methods are inclusive and accessible.

•  Employers and markets provide viable opportunities for trained participants.

•  Participants receive adequate post-training mentorship, placement, market linkage, and enterprise support.                                           • 	TechnoServe funds remain open and eligibility criteria are clear to CEL and enterprises.

•  Trained participants cannot access employment, start-up resources, productive assets, finance, or markets.

•  Persons with disabilities face inaccessible training environments and inadequate reasonable accommodation.

•  Participants drop out because of financial, household, mobility, accessibility, or caregiving constraints.

•  Training does not meet employer, market, or enterprise requirements.                                                                                            • 	Referral does not guarantee funding; TechnoServe decisions may be delayed or declined.

• 	Enterprises are referred before they are ready, or dropped from the pipeline.

### 3. Output / 3.3 Network (WAN) and Leadership

**Key result statement:** Established an inclusive and functional WAN that provides leadership development, mentorship, networking and support to young women and PWDs in aquaculture value chains

**Assumptions:**

•  Young women and persons with disabilities are willing and able to participate actively in WAN activities.

•  WAN maintains inclusive, representative, transparent, and accountable governance.

•  Qualified mentors and industry professionals remain available and committed to supporting members.

•  Network activities, services, and communication platforms are accessible and responsive to members’ diverse needs.

•  WAN secures adequate resources, partnerships, and institutional support for continued operation.

**Risks:**

•  Leadership disputes, elite capture, or inadequate representation of young women and persons with disabilities may undermine trust and ownership.

•  Limited mentor availability, poor matching, or inconsistent engagement may reduce the value of mentorship and leadership development.

•  Inaccessible activities, discrimination, harassment, or unsafe networking environments may exclude or harm some members.

•  Insufficient resources, partnerships, and institutional ownership may make WAN dependent on project funding and unable to continue functioning over time.

•  Some members may be unable to participate because of accessibility, mobility, caregiving, financial, or communication barriers.
